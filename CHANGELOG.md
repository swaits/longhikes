## 2026-11-17 - A ferry or a bus does not rule a trail out

- The inclusion rule said a trail must be walkable from one end to the other today. It now says more plainly that this is a judgement the maintainer makes case by case, not a mechanical test. A trail must be one route a person can travel from one end to the other on foot. A ferry, shuttle or bus link across water or a closed stretch, named by the holder or the route's author as the way through, does not rule a trail out. The record says how each such link is made, and the track has a gap there. A trail published only as separate pieces, a network with no through route, or a gap with no way across is still left out. Road links the holder publishes as the way through, and evidence that people have walked a trail end to end, still count. The wording is changed in the README, the contributing guide, the schema, the refresh rules and the method pages.
- The case that prompted it is the Washington Coast Trail, which stays: its author took a shuttle round Willapa Bay and buses at Taholah, Queets and Forks.
- No trail's facts changed.

## 2026-11-16 - A trail's length is one end-to-end walk, and a trail must be walkable from end to end

- The Florida Trail was shown as 1,500 miles. That is the holder's figure for the whole trail system, both branches at two splits plus connectors. One walk from end to end is about 1,130 miles, and an audit found about twenty records with the same kind of defect. The rule is now written down. A trail's length (`length_km`) is the end-to-end length of its canonical path: one branch at a split, no side trails, no stretch counted twice and no system total. It is the holder's own figure for that walk. `docs/schema.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, the inclusion rule in `README.md`, the glossary and the method page say so, and so do the monthly refresh's rules, record guide and two briefs, so that checking a record now includes checking that its length is one walk.
- A trail must also be walkable from one end to the other today. A trail published in separate pieces, or with a gap that has no way across, is not listed. Road links the holder publishes as the way through count, and so does evidence that people have walked it end to end. A trail that can be walked through but is partly unbuilt stays, with the holder's end-to-end route as its length and the built length beside it.
- Three new optional fields. `system_km` is the larger figure a holder publishes for a whole system or a planned total, and `system_note` says what it counts. `built_km` is the built length of an unfinished trail as the holder states it. The validator checks that the larger figure exceeds `length_km` and has its note, and that `built_km` is no more than `length_km` and sits only on a trail that says it is unfinished. ## 2026-11-16 - A monthly refresh process for the whole dataset

- The monthly refresh is now written down and can be started with one command. An agent follows the runbook in `.claude/skills/refresh-trails/` (start it with `/refresh-trails`, or `/refresh-trails light` to verify and re-check only). It verifies every record against the pages it cites, re-checks `left-out.toml` and its `waiting` entries, searches for missing trails and routes by region, looks at tracks and emblems, has every proposed change reviewed by a second reader who opens the cited pages, and stops for the maintainer's ruling on borderline trails before anything is applied.
- Eight briefs, one for each kind of work, and two shared files hold the rules the workers follow: evidence from the holder's or author's own page, no work around a site that refuses automated reads, no stopping a process by name, one Overpass request at a time. The staged proposal format is defined once, in `proposal-format.md`.
- A new tool, `tools/refresh.py`, does the mechanical parts with the standard library only. `slices` splits the trails and the left-out entries into balanced slices (`--since DAYS` takes only records whose `hike.checked` is older). `links` fetches every address in every record once, with a plain User-Agent, a delay per site and no retry of a refusal, into a CSV. `collect` gathers what the workers staged into one summary. `apply` makes the accepted proposals by targeted edits to the named fields, adds the `[[history]]` entry, and refuses a proposal whose old value no longer matches. It never rewrites a record.
- Staged work lives in `refresh/<date>/`, which `.gitignore` lists. Workers do not edit the repository; only the apply step does.
- `CONTRIBUTING.md` has a short section on the refresh. There is no change to any trail record or to the site.
